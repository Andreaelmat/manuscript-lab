# Known issues: The Lantern House

Answer key for `examples/benchmark/the-lantern-house.md`. Do not open this file while producing a review. Open it only after the review and the verification report are finished.

The fixture is short on purpose. A review that finds every plant and also files many generic complaints is not a successful review. Lines marked `quote:` are exact excerpts from the fixture.

## Planted problems

### K1. Weekday contradiction

- Kind: factual continuity
- Where: chapter 1 and chapter 2
- What a review should say: the narration states that the same date is both Tuesday and Wednesday. Nothing later corrects the weekday.
- quote: The rain reached the coast on Tuesday, 12 March, and turned the lane to the inn into a strip of black mud.
- quote: On Wednesday, 12 March, the rain had not stopped.

### K2. Eye color

- Kind: factual character attribute
- Where: chapter 1 and chapter 3
- What a review should say: Elena's eyes are green, then blue, with no disguise, time jump, or figurative reading that the text supports.
- quote: Her green eyes stung from the salt wind.
- quote: Her blue eyes were bloodshot, and the crack in the glass split the left one in two.

### K3. Useless repetition of the founding year

- Kind: interpretive repetition
- Where: chapter 2 and chapter 3
- What a review should say: the brass plate in chapter 1 gives the year a job. The later restatements add no pressure, information, or voice. The guest says it twice in one speech.
- quote: The inn had been founded in 1888. A brass plate said so beside the key rack, and Tomas wiped it with his sleeve as if the date might smear.
- quote: The inn had been founded in 1888.
- quote: The inn was founded in 1888.
- quote: Founded in 1888, yes.

The first of those four quotations is the functional mention. Filing it alone, without the later restatements, misses K3.

## Non-issues

### K4. Marco's drowning and Marco's letter

Chapter 1 says Marco drowned and could not swim. Chapter 2 shows a letter from Marco. Chapter 4 says he is alive in the attic and that Tomas invented the drowning. After chapter 4 this is a revealed concealment, not an open continuity error. A review that still lists it as an unresolved contradiction has a false positive. A review may describe the apparent clash and its resolution.

- quote: My brother Marco drowned in the inlet
- quote: I am in the capital. Do not tell them I can still write. — Marco
- quote: Marco was in the attic, alive, with ink on his fingers.
- quote: Tomas had invented the drowning for strangers who asked too many questions.

### K5. The attic bargain

The exchange from "We are full" through the unspoken price is a complete scene: Elena wants a room, Tomas wants the ledger copied, and neither restates the bargain. A review should not recommend rewriting it. Recording it as a strength is appropriate. Calling it thin, expository, or in need of a clearer conflict is a false positive unless the review identifies a specific failure this key does not see and quotes it.

- quote: Elena set her wet gloves on the ledger and waited.

### K6. The earlier Monday

The Monday in chapter 2 is a flashback to a previous winter. It does not conflict with Tuesday, 12 March.

- quote: That Monday was months ago.

### K7. The attic key

Tomas offers the attic, Elena later has the key in her pocket, and she puts it on the crate. That sequence is consistent.

- quote: the attic key was still in her pocket.

## What the fixture cannot prove

Finding K1 through K3, handling K4 through K7 correctly, and keeping the file unchanged is the expected behavior of one careful review. It does not demonstrate that the skill will edit a different novel well. Interpretive findings beyond this key can be legitimate when they are local, evidenced, and useful. Generic advice is not.
