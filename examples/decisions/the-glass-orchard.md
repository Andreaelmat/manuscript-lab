# Chapter 1. The Ticket

Lena reached the river with the night train ticket still in her fist. The number printed on it was 14 March, her mother's birthday. She had come to reach Iva before the hospital doors shut, and to learn what the orchard still held.

She threw the ticket into the river. The paper turned once and sank. She knew the train would leave without her. She also knew she could not hold that number in her hand and keep walking.

# Chapter 2. The Unread Fire

Pell caught her wrist above the camp stove. "That letter is the only account of the orchard," he said. "Burn it and you burn the message you came to learn."

Lena held the page to the flame until the writing curled. She did not answer him. The goal she had named at the river was still the goal, and the paper was gone.

# Chapter 3. The Ice

The footbridge was a dark rib over the water. The ice along its edges was thin enough to show the current.

Iva was on the far bank, and the hospital doors closed at midnight. Lena stepped onto the bridge. Her hands shook. She wanted her sister more than she wanted the dry road.

# Chapter 4. The Lit Road

Pell stood at the fork with a lantern. "The lit road is longer and it holds," he said. "You have walked it. It will still be there in the morning."

"I know that road," Lena said. "It passes my mother's house. I will not go in, and I will not walk past the door."

She took the dark track away from the lantern.

# Chapter 5. The Side Door

The orchard gate was chained. The side door stood open at the left of the wall. Lena had used that door every summer, and she saw it open now.

She climbed the broken wall instead. Her palms tore on the brick. She did not look at the door again.

# Chapter 6. The Chain

A second gate crossed the path inside the wall. It was chained, and Lena said the only key was in town with Pell.

She was among the trees. The grass was dry under her shoes. No hand undid the chain. No gap in the hedge was named.

# Chapter 7. Why the Paper Burned

In the trees she told Pell the part she had kept back. "I read the letter yesterday, until I could say it without the paper. I burned it so you could not take it off me on the road."

The message was already in her mouth. The fire had been a way to keep it there.
