# Known issues: The Glass Orchard

Answer key for `examples/decisions/the-glass-orchard.md`. Do not open this file while producing a review.

This fixture was written by the same project that maintains the skill. A review by that same agent is contaminated. The scenes are not taken from another novel, and findings about another novel are not a key for this file.

Lines marked `quote:` are exact excerpts. A credible irrational act, a conscious risk, and a conscious refusal are not defects. File a finding only for the ignored door and the vanished chain.

## Not defects

### D1. The ticket in the river

- kind: credible-irrational
- chapters: 1
- What a review should say: throwing away the ticket is costly and not optimal. The birthday on the paper is on the page, and so is her knowledge that the train will leave. Do not file a finding.
- quote: She threw the ticket into the river.
- quote: She knew the train would leave without her.

### D2. The ice bridge

- kind: emotional-risk
- chapters: 3
- What a review should say: she knows the ice is thin and crosses because she wants her sister before the doors close. The risk is conscious and the motive is on the page. Do not file a finding.
- quote: The ice along its edges was thin enough to show the current.
- quote: She wanted her sister more than she wanted the dry road.

### D3. The lit road

- kind: conscious-refusal
- chapters: 4
- What a review should say: the longer lit road is offered, she says she knows it, and she refuses it because it passes her mother's house. A safer alternative she knows and rejects is not a defect.
- quote: The lit road is longer and it holds
- quote: I know that road
- quote: I will not go in, and I will not walk past the door.

### D4. The burned letter, explained later

- kind: resolved-later
- chapters: 2, 7
- What a review should say: burning the only account looks like a refusal of her own goal. Chapter 7 says she had already memorized it and burned it so Pell could not take it. Do not leave the burning as an open motivation failure.
- quote: That letter is the only account of the orchard
- quote: I read the letter yesterday, until I could say it without the paper. I burned it so you could not take it off me on the road.

## Defects

### D5. The open side door

- kind: ignored-alternative
- chapters: 5
- What a review should say: she sees the side door open, and the narration says she has used it every summer. She climbs the broken wall instead. No fear, refusal, or later explanation supports avoiding the door.
- quote: The side door stood open at the left of the wall.
- quote: Lena had used that door every summer, and she saw it open now.
- quote: She climbed the broken wall instead.

### D6. The chained gate

- kind: obstacle-unexplained
- chapters: 6
- What a review should say: the inner gate is chained and the only key is in town. The next movement puts her among the trees. No hand, tool, gap, or time skip opens the chain.
- quote: It was chained, and Lena said the only key was in town with Pell.
- quote: She was among the trees.
- quote: No hand undid the chain. No gap in the hedge was named.

## What this key cannot prove

Separating D1–D4 from D5–D6 is the expected shape of one careful developmental pass. It does not show that the same distinction will hold in another book. Do not treat a match with a review of another book as evidence about this fixture.
