# Editorial methodology

Use this file for a full review, and whenever you need the standard that the specialized files share. Close it before opening another reference.

## What this review is

Judge the book as a composed narrative. Look for structure, character pressure, pacing, dialogue, continuity, information, openings, endings, and theme. Do not proofread spelling, and do not line-edit for taste.

A working passage is a result. Record it as a `strength` with priority `none` when naming it helps the author see what to protect. Do not file a defect because a review is expected to contain defects.

## Facts and interpretations

A factual finding shows passages that cannot all be true in the story world after flashback, deliberate concealment, and an unreliable narrator have been accounted for. Quote every side.

An interpretive finding says what the text does to attention, sympathy, or meaning, and admits that a careful reader could disagree. Put that admission in `uncertainty`.

Do not upgrade an interpretation to a fact because you feel sure.

## How to read

1. Read the index: order, chapter titles, word counts, and segmentation notes.
2. Build a narrative map while reading the original paragraphs: who wants what, what opposes them, what changes, and what the reader knows that the characters do not. Store paragraph ids in the map.
3. For `survey`, follow the depth rule in `SKILL.md` and do not pretend the unread middle was judged.
4. For `standard` and `deep`, read every in-scope paragraph before you judge the book as a whole.
5. When a claim depends on a passage, read that passage again from `index.json`. Do not cite a summary.
6. After the findings file validates and the verifier accepts the quotations, use the critical-verification procedure on each finding you intend to keep.

Hierarchical reading is a way to move through a long book. It is not permission to judge the book from notes alone.

## What not to impose

Do not require a three-act shape, a hero's journey, a beat sheet, or a twist. A quiet book can be complete. A genre book can be exacting. Judge the book against the effects it is actually producing.

Do not confuse motif with waste. A repeated image that gains pressure, irony, or new information can be doing work. A repeated fact that changes nothing is a repetition finding, and it is interpretive unless the repetition also creates a factual contradiction.

## Scene standard

Use this test when a scene is under discussion. It is not a quota.

- Somebody wants something in the scene, even if the want is to avoid a subject.
- Something resists that want.
- The situation at the end is different in fact, knowledge, or leverage.
- The scene is legible without an explanation that restates it.

A scene can pass this test and still be the wrong scene for its position in the book. Say which of those you mean.

## Openings and endings

An opening has a limited job: put a person in a situation that can sustain attention, and teach the reader how to read the voice. Do not demand a death, a thesis, or a full explanation of the world.

An ending fails when it abandons a pressure the book taught the reader to track, or when it solves that pressure with a fact the book withheld unfairly. Ambiguity is not abandonment when the book has been honest about what it withholds.

## Information

Track three layers: what the narrator states, what a character believes, and what the reader can infer. A mystery is a managed gap. A cheat is a gap that makes an earlier scene unreadable once the answer arrives. Name which one you claim, and quote the setup and the payoff.

## Priorities

Use `high` when a reader loses orientation, causality, or a reason to trust the narration, and the text does not support that loss as a designed effect. Use `medium` when a stretch remains readable but a central scene, choice, or relationship is thinner than the book needs. Use `low` for a local option. Use `none` for a strength.

Do not use `high` for a sentence you would have written differently.

## Interventions

Describe the narrative job the change would do. Do not supply a rewritten paragraph, scene, or ending. "Cut the second statement of the founding year" is an intervention. A substitute sentence is not.

## Limits you always record

Record the mode, depth, report language, unread or out-of-scope text, whether segmentation was ambiguous, the verifier counts, whether an independent model was used, and the model identity if the host exposes it. If it does not, write "unspecified." State that the review is not a score and not a market valuation.
