# Developmental editing

Use this file while judging structure, tension, scene function, motivation, or the handling of revelations. Do not use it as a continuity inventory or a line edit.

## Structure

Describe the structure you find before you compare it with any template. Useful questions:

- What is the central pressure, and where does it become visible?
- Which scenes change a relationship, a plan, or the reader's understanding?
- Which scenes repeat a situation without changing the terms?
- Where does the book promise an outcome it neither pays nor deliberately withholds?

A long middle is not a defect by itself. A middle in which the same obstacle is cleared in the same way, more than once, is a structural observation. Quote the repeated move and say what does not change.

Do not prescribe three acts, a midpoint reversal, or a climax on a schedule. If the book uses one of those shapes, you may say whether that shape is doing work. If it does not, judge the shape it has.

## Scene function

Relate the scene to the book, not only to itself.

- What does this scene make possible later, or what later scene does it fail to set up?
- If the scene were removed, which later decision would become unearned?
- Is the scene carrying exposition that a later conflict should carry instead?

A well-built local scene can still be unnecessary to the book. Say that only when you can name the later passage that does not need it. A scene that is locally inert is a different claim. Do not merge them.

The attic-bargain kind of scene, in which two people want different things and leave the price unspoken, can be complete. Do not file it because it is short or because it lacks an argument.

## Tension and pace

Tension is unresolved pressure, not constant volume. Look for:

- a want that is delayed, refused, or made costly
- a stretch in which no want is active and no information changes
- a release that arrives before the pressure has been felt
- acceleration that skips the choice the ending needs

Name the paragraphs where the pressure starts, stalls, and changes. "The pacing is slow" is not a finding.

## Motivation

Ask what this person knows at this moment, what they want, and what it costs them to act. A choice is weakly motivated when the text has given the person a reason not to make it and no pressure that overcomes that reason.

Fear, habit, loyalty, and self-deception are motives. Do not require every choice to be strategically optimal. Do require the book to show the force that wins. An irrational, selfish, or self-destructive choice can be credible. A safer road the person knows and refuses is not, by itself, a defect. A motive that arrives later is not an open failure. File a motivation finding only when the scene still does not support the choice, or when a concrete obstacle disappears without a means on the page.

For a full or developmental review, the optional record is `decision-audit.json`. The procedure is in `decision-audit.md` in this folder. Check it with `scripts/verify_decision_audit.py`. Use the fact ledger to separate what the reader knows from what the character knows. Do not score the decision.

Do not invent a psychological history the text does not contain. If the motive is missing on the page, that absence is the finding.

## Revelations

Separate fair surprise from a cheat.

- Fair: the earlier scene can be reread and still means what it meant, with an added fact.
- Costly: the revelation forces a character to act differently, or shows why they cannot.
- Flat: the revelation explains a mystery the scenes were not actually about.
- Unfair: an earlier scene only makes sense if the reader already knew the later fact, and the book presented that scene as readable.

Quote the setup and the payoff. If a later chapter resolves an apparent contradiction, the contradiction is not a developmental failure. Say that the apparent break is part of the design, or do not file it.

## Causality

Coincidence that complicates a situation is different from coincidence that removes the problem. Flag the second when the book has not prepared it. Do not flag chance itself.

## What to leave alone

Leave sentence rhythm, dialect, and metaphor to the dialogue-and-style pass unless they block the structural claim. Leave date tables and object tracking to the continuity pass unless a broken fact also breaks cause and effect. If it does, you may file one factual finding and label it as continuity inside a developmental review.
