# Character analysis

Use this file when a finding is about who a person is, what they want, or how they change. Dialogue mechanics live in the dialogue-and-style reference. Open that file only while you are judging voice.

## What to describe first

For each person who carries the observation, state only what the text supports:

- the want visible in the present action
- the fear, loyalty, or habit that competes with it
- what they know
- what they do at cost to themselves
- how another person treats them, and whether that treatment changes

Separate a label the narration gives them from a trait their actions demonstrate. If those diverge, that divergence may be the finding, or it may be the point.

## Change and consistency

Development is a change the book tracks. A contradiction is a trait the book treats as stable while stating it in incompatible ways.

"She becomes willing to stay" is development when earlier she was leaving and the scenes earn the turn. "Her eyes are green" and later "her eyes are blue," with no change of person, light-as-metaphor, or disguise, is a factual continuity problem. File that kind of clash with the continuity standard, category `character` or `continuity`, judgment `factual`.

Do not require every person to have an arc. A stable antagonist, a chorus figure, or a person who refuses to change can be exact. File a missing arc only when the book promises a turn and then skips the choice that would make it.

## Relationships

Track a relationship as a series of changes in leverage, trust, or knowledge. One quarrel is not an arc. A relationship finding needs the scene where the terms are established and the scene where they are different, or the absence of that second scene when the ending depends on it.

Do not invent offstage history. If the book needs a history it never put on the page, the absence is the finding.

## Credibility

A person acts out of character when the act cancels a desire or fear the book has made specific, and no pressure in the scene is strong enough to override it. Quote the established desire and the act. Consider, before you file, that people also act from vanity, panic, and contradictory wants. Those are not automatic defects.

## Secondary people

A secondary person may exist to exert one pressure. That is enough. File a complaint only when the book asks the reader to believe a complexity it has not written, or when the person knows or does something the surrounding scenes forbid.

## Voice

Note voice here only when it changes what the person is willing to say, hide, or misunderstand. Lexicon, rhythm, and indistinguishability belong to the dialogue pass. Do not file both for the same sentences unless the structural effect and the stylistic effect are different. If they are the same effect, keep one finding and name both consequences in `narrative_impact`.

## Strengths

When a person's want, obstacle, and choice are legible without a character sketch, you may record a strength. Cite the choice. Do not add a suggestion to make the person "more relatable" or more explicit.
