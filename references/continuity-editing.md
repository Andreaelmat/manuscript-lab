# Continuity editing

Use this file to find incompatibilities between passages. A continuity review does not restyle prose and does not demand a different plot.

## Build a ledger

While reading, record claims you may need to find again in `narrative-ledger.json`. The procedure is in `narrative-ledger.md` in this same folder. Check the file with `scripts/verify_ledger.py`. A prose list is not a substitute: the structured file is what a later pass can search by paragraph id. The ledger does not decide that two claims conflict, and it does not replace the paragraphs.

Track only what the text states:

- dates, weekdays, and times of day
- ages and durations
- who is alive, present, or absent
- family and other relationships
- objects and where they are
- places and travel time
- injuries, clothing, and other body facts
- what each person knows, and when they learn it

Do not fill a gap with a plausible fact. A gap is not a contradiction.

## What counts as a contradiction

File a factual continuity finding when two narrator statements cannot both be true, or when an event is told in incompatible ways, and no later passage resolves the clash.

Quote both sides. Name the chapters. Say which fact would have to change.

Use interpretive judgment when the text might be a character's mistake, a lie, a memory, or a designed gap, and you cannot settle it from the words alone. Say which reading you prefer and why it might be wrong.

## Do not treat these as errors

- A flashback with its own time. Quote the cue that places it in the past.
- A mystery the book later answers. Quote the answer. If you noticed the clash before the answer, the finished review rejects it as an open error.
- A lie a character tells, when the book shows it is a lie or gives the character a reason to lie and does not confirm the lie in narration.
- An unreliable narrator, when the book gives contradictory versions and a way to see the unreliability. If the book seems unaware of its own clash, the clash can still be factual.
- A repeated telling that adds a new detail without cancelling the old one.
- Different names for the same place when the text equates them.

## Knowledge

A character cannot use a fact they have not learned unless the narration is reporting error or deceit. Quote the scene where they lack the fact and the scene where they use it. Check for an intervening scene before you file.

The reader may know more than the character. That is dramatic irony, not a continuity error.

## Objects, bodies, and time

Follow an object that matters: who holds it, when it is given away, and whether it is still there. One missed cup is not worth a finding. A key, a letter, or a weapon that is in two places at once is.

Body facts include eye color, scars, handedness, age, and injuries. A change the book treats as change is development. A change the book treats as the same unchanged fact is a contradiction.

Duration must be possible. A person cannot finish a journey the book has already timed as longer, unless the book is unreliable on purpose. Do not invent travel times the book never states.

## Apparent contradictions

Before you file, search the later in-scope text for a passage that explains the clash. If you find one, do not leave the clash in the report as an open defect. You may mention it in the continuity section as a pressure that the book resolves, with both the clash and the resolution quoted.

If the scope stops before the likely resolution, say that the finding is provisional and name the unread portion.

## Priority

A broken weekday or an impossible double location is `high` when the reader must use it to understand what happened. It is `medium` when the plot remains clear and the fact is still visibly wrong. Do not file `high` for a detail the book never asks the reader to track, unless the detail is repeated as if it mattered.
